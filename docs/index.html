One small CNN, three attention tasks

This page runs a small convolutional neural network in your browser. It was trained on three classic attention tasks at once (Posner cueing, visual search and contextual cueing) and is never told which task an image belongs to: it has to tell from the picture. Pick an image from the paper, draw your own or upload one, and see what the network answers and what each layer responds to.

Architecture and stimuli from Srivastava, Yang & Eckstein (2024). Code, methods and results: github.com/WojciechMa/brainhack-cnn.

New here? What this page shows

The three tasks

  • Posner cueing: a cue box marks one side; is a target line present? The cue is predictive: the target appears on the cued side on 80 % of trials (valid). With such a cue people detect targets better on the cued side. With uninformative cues this benefit turns into a cost after about 300 ms (inhibition of return), which a network without a sense of time cannot show.
  • Visual search: is the target present at one of the places marked by boxes? With more boxes (1 to 8) the search gets harder.
  • Contextual cueing: which way is the target tilted? "Old" layouts were repeated during training, "new" ones were not. People find targets faster in repeated layouts.

The network

Three convolution layers (12, 14 and 18 feature maps), then 600 Dense units, then 6 outputs: target or no target for Posner and for search, tilted left or right for contextual cueing. It learned from noisy images, so the noise slider adds the same kind of noise to your image.

Like people, it shows all three attention effects: it does better after a valid cue, with fewer boxes and in old layouts.

The panels

  • Answer: the strongest of the six outputs and its probability. For the paper's images it also says whether the answer is right.
  • Inside the network: what each layer responds to; brighter means stronger. Point at a map or unit to see its value.
  • Lesions: switch off a group of Dense units and see how the three effects change. Intact is the whole network, No … units the network without the chosen group, No random units the same number of random units switched off, as a control. If the group row drops and the random row does not, those units carry that effect.

Live trials in your browser

Runs test images of the paper with new noise, the same number from every condition, and measures the three effects here. Once trials have run, changing the lesion rescores them at once.

Effects in percentage points of AUC (the chance that the network rates a random target image above a random no-target image; for contextual cueing, a right-tilted T above a left-tilted one): Posner cueing = valid − invalid trials, set size = 1 − 8 boxes, contextual cueing = old − new layouts.

Inside the network

Brighter = stronger response (scaled to the strongest unit in each layer). Hover over a map or a unit for its values.

What the network sees: 224 × 224 with noise

Conv 1: 12 maps, 111 × 111

Conv 2: 14 maps, 55 × 55

Conv 3: 18 maps, 54 × 54

Dense: 600 units

Self-test

Network: Conv(12, 3×3, stride 2) → Conv(14, 3×3, stride 2) → Conv(18, 2×2) → BatchNorm → Dense(600) → 6 outputs, the joint network of Srivastava, Yang & Eckstein (2024), Emergent human-like covert attention in feedforward convolutional neural networks, Current Biology. Trained from scratch by us (seed 0 of 10). The Dense weights are stored with 8 bits each to keep the download small.
